\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5white,colframe=primaryblue,title=\textbf{Executive Overview}]
The domestic workforce sector in India represents a \$10B+ unorganized market operating largely through informal word-of-mouth recommendations and predatory middlemen. \textbf{Sevikaa} was initiated to solve critical market failures: severe trust deficits in gated communities, time-slot mismatches, digital literacy barriers, and middleman exploitation of vulnerable domestic workers.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Industry Context \& Market Fragmentation}
In urban India, domestic services (maids, cooks, nannies) are essential to daily household operation. However, the market remains highly fragmented:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Informal Dominance:} Over 90\% of hiring occurs without verified background checks or digital contracts.
    \item \textbf{Predatory Middlemen:} Local placement agencies charge exorbitant upfront fees to employers and take cuts from worker salaries without guaranteeing background safety or job security.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Technology Infrastructure:} Lack of tailored mobile-first platforms for informal sector workers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Demand-Side Pain Points (Employers \& Gated Societies)}

\subsection{Trust \& Security Gap}
Urban residents and gated society management committees face safety risks when admitting unverified personnel into private homes. Independent police verification is cumbersome and rarely executed by individual employers.

\subsection{Time-Slot Incompatibility}
Household routines require help at specific times (e.g., meal prep between 7:00--8:30 AM). Traditional hiring provides zero visibility into worker availability, leading to frequent schedule clashes.

\subsection{Exploitative Agency Commissions}
Unregulated agencies charge employers upfront fees equivalent to 1--2 months of worker salary without offering background validity or retention guarantees.

\section{Supply-Side Pain Points (Domestic Workers)}

\subsection{Agent Exploitation}
Domestic workers—predominantly women—frequently pay upfront fees to agents or surrender portions of their monthly earnings.

\subsection{Language \& Digital Literacy Barriers}
Conventional job applications cater to English-speaking white-collar professionals. Domestic workers encounter severe digital navigation hurdles.

\subsection{Lack of Portable Work Reputation}
Workers build years of experience but possess no formal work history or digital profile to prove credentials to prospective employers.

\section{The Sevikaa Solution}

Sevikaa addresses these core problems through five architectural pillars:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Curated Multi-Stage Trust Verification:} Mandatory Aadhaar verification, Police clearance check, Telephonic onboarding interview, and Video Introduction clip audit.
    \item \textbf{Hyper-Local Society-Centric Matching:} Database indexing anchored to gated apartment societies, prioritizing nearby candidates to minimize commute friction.
    \item \textbf{Granular Availability Engine:} Weekly time-slot matrix enabling precise routine matching.
    \item \textbf{100\% Free Model for Workers:} Zero commissions or registration fees charged to workers; sustainable revenue derived from employer contact subscriptions.
    \item \textbf{Multilingual \& Tele-Onboarding Support:} Platform support in 8 Indian regional languages alongside phone-guided onboarding.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Socio-Economic Impact \& Scalability Vision}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Women Empowerment:} Direct economic access and fair wage visibility for female domestic workers.
    \item \textbf{Labor Formalization:} Transitioning informal workers into recognized service professionals with portable digital reputations.
    \item \textbf{Community Security:} Enhancing safety standards across urban gated apartment complexes.
\end{itemize}
